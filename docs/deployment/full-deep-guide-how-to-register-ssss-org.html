1 — What Is a Domain & How It Works

Before buying anything, it helps to understand what you are actually purchasing.

A domain name like ssss.org is a human-readable address that maps to a server's IP address (e.g. 75.2.60.5). Without a domain, your visitors would need to type a raw IP number into their browser — which nobody does.

The Domain Name System (DNS) is a global phone book. When someone types www.ssss.org, their browser asks a DNS server: "What IP is this?" DNS answers, and the browser connects to that IP.

A registrar is an accredited company (like Namecheap or GoDaddy) that is authorised to sell and manage domain registrations on behalf of ICANN — the global authority that oversees domain names.

.org is a top-level domain (TLD) historically used by non-profit organisations and societies — which makes it perfect for the Soil Science Society of Syria.

The chain of ownership looks like this:

ICANN (global authority)
  └─► Public Interest Registry (manages .org TLD)
        └─► Namecheap / GoDaddy / Porkbun (registrars — sell to you)
              └─► YOU (domain owner — registrant)
                    └─► DNS records → point to Netlify + Railway

WHOIS / Registrant Contact Information

What is WHOIS? Every domain has a public record called WHOIS that lists who owns it — your name, email, address, and phone number. This is a legal requirement by ICANN.

This is a privacy risk. Spammers and data scrapers harvest WHOIS data constantly.

Solution: Enable WHOIS Privacy Protection (also called Domain Privacy or Private Registration). This replaces your personal details in the public record with the registrar's proxy contact details. Your real information stays private but is still on file with the registrar for legal purposes.

At Porkbun, WHOIS Privacy is always free. Make sure it is enabled (it is on by default).

7 — What You Own After Purchase

What "owning a domain" actually means:

You do not own the domain like you own a car. You have the exclusive right to use it for the period you paid for (e.g. 2 years). After that period, if you do not renew, anyone else can register it.

What you can do with your domain:
• Point it at any server anywhere in the world using DNS records
• Create email addresses @ssss.org
• Transfer it to another registrar
• Sell it or transfer ownership to someone else
• Protect it from being transferred by enabling the "registrar lock"

What you cannot do:
• You cannot own it forever without paying annual renewal fees
• You cannot own the same name under a different TLD automatically (owning ssss.org does NOT give you ssss.com)

9 — Point the Domain at Your Hosting (DNS)

What is DNS?
DNS (Domain Name System) is where you tell the internet: "When someone visits ssss.org, send them to THIS server." You do this by adding DNS records in your registrar's dashboard.

Types of DNS records you need:
• A record — points a domain name to an IPv4 address (e.g. ssss.org → 75.2.60.5)
• CNAME record — points a domain name to another domain name (e.g. www.ssss.org → your-site.netlify.app)
• MX record — for email routing (only needed if you set up email @ssss.org)
• TXT record — for verification purposes (Netlify, Google, etc. ask you to add these to prove ownership)

DNS propagation: After you add or change a DNS record, it takes 5 minutes to 48 hours to "propagate" worldwide. During this time some users may see the old address, others the new one. This is normal.

10 — Connect Domain to Netlify (Frontend)

Your Next.js frontend is hosted on Netlify. This is where ssss.org and www.ssss.org should point.

5

Add the domain in Netlify

  1. Log in to app.netlify.com
  2. Click on your site (the SSSSY frontend site)
  3. Go to Site Configuration → Domain Management
  4. Click Add a domain
  5. Type ssss.org and click Verify
  6. Also add www.ssss.org
  7. Netlify will show you the DNS records to add — note them down
Netlify will show you something like:

• For ssss.org → add an A record pointing to 75.2.60.5
• For www.ssss.org → add a CNAME record pointing to your-site-name.netlify.app

The exact values are shown in your Netlify dashboard — use those exact values.
6

Add DNS records at your registrar (Porkbun)

  1. Go to porkbun.com → log in → click Domain Management
  2. Find ssss.org in your list → click DNS or Manage DNS
  3. Add the records Netlify gave you:
TypeHost / NameValue / Points toTTL
A @ (means root: ssss.org) 75.2.60.5 (Netlify's IP — check your dashboard) 300
CNAME www (means www.ssss.org) your-site.netlify.app 300
What is TTL? Time To Live — how long (in seconds) DNS resolvers should cache this record. 300 = 5 minutes. A lower TTL means changes propagate faster (useful when setting up), a higher TTL means less DNS traffic (better once stable). You can increase it to 3600 (1 hour) after everything is working.
  1. Click Save after adding each record
  2. Wait 5–30 minutes for DNS to propagate
7

HTTPS / SSL Certificate — Automatic

Once Netlify detects your DNS records are pointing to it, it will automatically issue a free SSL certificate from Let's Encrypt. This gives you the green padlock and https:// in the address bar.

You do not need to do anything. Netlify handles this entirely. It may take up to 1 hour after DNS propagates for the certificate to appear. After that, all HTTP traffic is automatically redirected to HTTPS.

You will see the status change to "Your site is secured with HTTPS" in the Netlify domain management panel.

11 — Connect Domain to Railway (Backend API)

Your Spring Boot backend runs on Railway. The frontend calls it at /api/*. You have two options:

OptionHowRequires
Option A (Recommended): Netlify Proxy Add a rewrite in netlify.toml so ssss.org/api/* proxies to Railway. No Railway custom domain needed. Free — already configured in your netlify.toml
Option B: Railway Custom Domain Add api.ssss.org as a custom domain in Railway. Add a CNAME at your registrar pointing api to Railway's URL. Railway Starter plan ($5/mo)
8

Option A — Netlify Proxy (Free, No Extra DNS)

Add these lines to your netlify.toml (already done in your codebase after the earlier update):

[[redirects]]
  from   = "/api/*"
  to     = "https://ssssy-websit-production.up.railway.app/api/:splat"
  status = 200
  force  = true

[[redirects]]
  from   = "/storage/*"
  to     = "https://ssssy-websit-production.up.railway.app/storage/:splat"
  status = 200
  force  = true
What this does: When the browser calls https://ssss.org/api/..., Netlify's servers silently forward that request to Railway and return the response. The browser never knows Railway is involved — it sees only ssss.org. This eliminates CORS issues and hides the Railway URL from users.

Then set in Netlify Environment Variables:

NEXT_PUBLIC_API_URL = https://ssss.org/api

13 — Renewal — Never Lose Your Domain

Domains expire. If you forget to renew, your domain enters a grace period (usually 30 days) where you can still renew at normal price. After that it enters a "redemption period" (30–60 days) where renewal costs $100–$300. After that, it is released for anyone to register.

Protect yourself with these steps:

  1. Enable Auto-Renew in your registrar dashboard — this is the most important step
  2. Keep your payment method up to date — if your card expires and auto-renew fails, you could lose the domain
  3. Keep your email address active — the registrar sends renewal reminders 90, 60, 30, 15, and 7 days before expiry
  4. Register for multiple years — buying 2–3 years upfront reduces risk and often costs less per year
  5. Add a calendar reminder — set an annual reminder 60 days before expiry as a backup
When does ssss.org expire? Your registrar dashboard shows the exact expiry date. You can find it under "My Domains" → click on ssss.org → look for "Expiry Date" or "Renewal Date". Note it down somewhere safe.
